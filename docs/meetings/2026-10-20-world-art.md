# Level design / environment art review — My Dreamy RPG (Act I big maps)

Expert review, 2026-10-20 (level design + environment art direction), requested by the director on behalf of the client. Adopted.
Renders referenced below live in docs/meetings/world-art/ (A_vale_edge_sw.png, A_vale_trees.png (2x crop), B_tall_summit.png, B_tall_from_south.png, C_pine_r2_wall.png, C_pine_gutter_v.png). The B_* shots are a RUNTIME experiment: a 13-level stepped hill injected into the Vale in the page (no repo change) to test what the engine does with real height.

Measured facts the report leans on
- Iso tile 32x16 px, ZS = 6 px per height unit (js/util.js), scene 480x270 at 2x.
- Vale (80x80): 3228 land tiles; heights 0: 2694 (83 %), 2: 113, 4: 409 — one terrace, 24 px tall. 439 objects = 13.6 per 10x10 land tiles; 163 trees = 5.0 per 10x10.
- Pinewood (112x112): 6299 land tiles; levels used 0/2/4/6 only (6 = 36 px, the tallest thing in the game, roughly the hero's 34 px). 1047 objects = 16.6 per 10x10; 350 pines = 5.6 per 10x10; 5124 column sprites, 178 static lights.
- Chunk raster cost (desktop, 512 px chunks, measured): Vale 4–163 ms per chunk, Pinewood 25–240 ms. A chunk holding the 13-level hill (64 columns): 105 ms. Phones use 256 px chunks (1/4 the work). PRODUCTION.md: phone p95 53–66 ms under 4x throttle vs the 20 ms budget.
- Height data allows 0..35 (base36; gen clamps at 35 = 210 px). render.js viewTiles() pads only 190 px below the view, so a column whose base is more than ~29 units below a visible top gets culled: 28 units is the safe ceiling until that pad is raised.
- walkable(): |dh| <= 1.05. Generator defaults (gen.js MapGen): step 2 (terraces), slope 0.6 units/tile, amp 1.6.

## 1. Diagnosis — why each complaint happens (file:function)

### 1.1 "Not satisfied with the general look of the tile maps"
Root cause: there is no tile grammar, only noise.
- maps.js:surfaceColor — every ground pixel = palette ramp sampled by fbm(0.7/tile) + fbm(0.14/tile) + a PER-PIXEL hash2 jitter (±0.07) + per-pixel "flecks" (0.6 % of grass pixels and 3.5 % of meadow pixels become pink/white/yellow/blue). Per 480x270 frame that is several hundred to a few thousand isolated bright specks: the confetti/static over every Vale field (kbm-002, kbm-003). It destroys value separation, so everything reads as one textured plane.
- Tile-to-tile blending is a vnoise jitter of the TYPE lookup (surfaceColor lines 116–118): grass/meadow/moss bleed into each other with no edge, no transition tile, no shape. Paths ('p') are a Voronoi cobble that stops dead at the tile boundary.
- maps.js:rasterRect — surface, 12 px slab (SLAB) and underside are the only three vertical bands. No lip highlight, no rim shadow, no ambient occlusion where props or columns meet the ground.
- THEMES ramps are 5–7 straight lerps applied through rampDither; the palette is acceptable, the noise feeding it is the problem.
- render.js:drawScene — four full-screen passes (lightmap multiply at half res, additive halos, bloom at 1/4 res, soft-light GRADE at 22 %, vignette) flatten contrast further: the Vale ends as one bright lime plane over a saturated purple sky with nothing dark in the mid-ground.

### 1.2 "The trees are kind of ugly"
Two tree sources exist and the one the client sees most is the frozen one.
- art.js:applySprites(), called from buildArt() after main.js:loadSprites() (main.js:292–293), OVERWRITES ART.trees.round/poplar/blossom/teal/dead, ART.bush, ART.grass, ART.rock with the PNG data-URIs in js/sprites.js (tree_round 32x44, poplar 20x52, blossom 34x44, teal 28x40, dead 30x46; 4 variants each). So the Vale lollipops are the frozen sheets; art.js:blobTree is NOT what the player sees. A blobTree rewrite changes nothing until js/sprites.js is regenerated or the tree keys are dropped from the override loop.
- The sheets: a single ellipsoid canopy, a 4 px stick trunk, a 1 px dark outline all round, 3 value steps, no interior leaf clusters, no rim light, no branch structure, no ground contact. At 2x (A_vale_trees.png) they read as green balloons on sticks.
- Pines (art.js:pineTree) are live-procedural and better, but tiers are mirror-symmetric about cx; the sunset rim is hard-coded to the RIGHT edge while _forest.js scatters pines with flip:true (placeObjects: flip: !!o.f), so about half the pines carry their rim on the wrong side; the needle gaps are a single vnoise threshold (0.78) that cuts the same holes on every tier; only 3 giant / 4 tall / 3 young variants.
- Shadows: render.js drawScene shadow() = one flat ellipse (alpha 0.32, ratio 0.45) per object; nothing casts directionally, so trees do not sit on the ground.
- No sway: only ART.grass flips between 2 frames (drawItem it.grass). Trees are rigid.

### 1.3 "No real volume and no density"
Volume:
- maps.js:rasterColumn — a column is 32 x (16 + 6h + 1) px, two faces at lit 1.0/0.66, value falling 0.35 over the height, "strata" = one darker row every 6 px, vines on 14 % of columns. At h = 2 or 4 that is a 12–24 px wall with 2–4 strata rows: a cardboard step (C_pine_r2_wall.png, A_vale_terrace_cliff.png). No overhang, no cap lip, no base darkening, no cast shadow on the lower ground, no debris at the foot.
- Heights: 83 % of the Vale is flat; Pinewood uses 0/2/4/6 only. No skyline anywhere.
- Trees are whole sprites in one y-sort layer: the hero is never UNDER a canopy, so the forest has no ceiling.
Density:
- 5 trees per 10x10 on both maps (DESIGN 8b's 150–200 objects on a 56-tile map is ~10 per 10x10 LAND tiles; Pinewood hits 16.6 only because ~40 % are 9x8 grass tufts, mushrooms and 14 px rocks).
- _forest.js/_vale.js spacing: pine sp 1.5, giant 3.2, poplar 1.5, teal 1.6, round 2.2 — spacing alone caps the forest at ~1 tree per 2.3 tiles squared even where p() = 1. gen.js placeOk rejects anything with r > 0 within dPath < r + 0.55 of a corridor and inside fight zones, so corridors are bald.
- Ground clutter is pixel flecks, not sprites: no litter, roots, pebbles, needle mats or moss decals (OBJ_TYPES has the 'moss' mound and 'log' only).

### 1.4 "The map borders look strange and floaty"
- maps.js:groundPrepGen records only top[px] and edge[px]; rasterRect draws slab + underside BELOW edge only. In this projection (isoY = (x+y)*8 − z*6) a north- or west-facing face projects behind its own top, so the north and west coasts have NO visible thickness: the top surface is cut against the sky with a 1 px saw-tooth (A_vale_edge_sw.png upper-left, kbm-002 left edge). This is the single biggest floaty cue.
- The south/east underside is a uniform curtain: len = underDepth (150/140 px) x a centre-weighted falloff, fbm streaks, haze mix to 0.75, 8 % root strands, 1.5 % crystals. Its bottom is a near-straight line with no silhouette (no rock lobes, no stalactite clusters, roots too thin), so the island looks like a cut-out card.
- Nothing ties the island to the sky: no shadow on the cloud sea; the 3 "sea" clouds (150x46) are small; the far islets (render.js buildIslet, 30–46 px) sit ABOVE the horizon so scale is ambiguous; the far mountains float above the island's level on the left of A_vale_edge_sw.png.
- Section gutters: maps.js:gutterColor — 'v' = a 1.6-tile dark gradient from the north lip then under[0]*0.35; 'k' = flat near-black. gutter_hollow_edge.png / C_pine_gutter_v.png: a hard 3-tile black diagonal stripe with the sunset visible under it. The Hollow (hollow_1_arrival.png) is an island with sky behind it, contradicting "underground".
- Edge waterfalls (drawFall edge) help but fall 230 px with no spray at the lip and no mist at the bottom.

## 2. Art direction fix — within Canvas 2D and the phone budget

Rule for every item: if it does not move, it is rasterised into the chunk (maps.js rasterRect per pixel; sprites onto the chunk canvas after putImageData, the hook buildChunkStep already uses for drawCrystals) and cached/LRU'd. The live layer stays what it is (≤ 3 fog tiles, ≤ 40 lights, bloom, glints, falls) plus exactly three new live things: canopy alpha, hero silhouette, a capped set of cast-shadow sprites. Budget: the chunk raster may cost +40 % (Pinewood worst case 240 -> ~340 ms desktop; phone 256 px chunk ~85 ms, still time-sliced by groundTick's BAND rows).

### 2.1 Trees (art.js; then freeze into js/sprites.js)
One builder canopyTree(seed, spec) replacing blobTree:
- Trunk: 5–7 px at the base flaring to 9–11 px with 2–3 root toes (3 px each), tapering to 3 px at the first fork; 1 px dark side, 1 px warm lit side; bark ramp 5 steps; a knot from hash. Trunk = 35–45 % of sprite height for broadleaf, 45–60 % for pines.
- Canopy = 3 leaf masses back to front: shadow mass (2 blobs, darkest 2 ramp steps, offset 2 px down-left), mid mass (3–4 blobs), lit mass (2–3 smaller blobs, top-right, lightest 2 steps). Each blob an ellipse with a vnoise-eroded edge (erode 25 %), drawn as 3–5 px leaf clumps; ordered 2x2 dither only between adjacent ramp steps, never per-pixel hash.
- Rim light: 1 px on the lit side of EVERY cluster, colour = theme light (Vale #ffd6ec at 55 % mix, forest #d0904a). Build left-lit and right-lit sheets and pick by the theme's light side (Vale moon at x 470 = right; forest sun x 360 = centre-right); drop random flip for trees (keep it for rocks/logs).
- Outline: no full outline; a 1 px darker core-colour edge on the shadow side only.
- Sizes: 3 species per zone x 6 variants x 2 sizes (24x36, 36x64) = 36 sheets + 3 giants (64x140). ~330 KB of canvases per zone.
- Ground contact: a 4 px dark AO ellipse baked into the sprite base + the live directional shadow (2.2).
- Sway subset: split each tree into trunk + canopy sprites (needed for the canopy fade anyway). 1 in 4 trees gets a 3-frame canopy (top 60 % shifted −1/0/+1 px), phase from the existing `sway` field. One extra drawImage per swaying tree.
- Pines: per-tier reach random per side (0.6–1.1 independently), 6–9 tiers, a bare 3–5 px tip, 2–3 broken lower branches on giants, dust/snow highlight on tier tops in the rim pass.
- Freeze path: SPRITE_ART wants {frames, src: PNG data-URI} (loadSprites/applySprites), so the freeze is canvas.toDataURL('image/png') from the headless-Chromium tooling (tools/maps/shots-big.js pattern) written into js/sprites.js — NOT canvasToSprite, which emits the editor's {w,h,pal,rows} format for world.sprites. Alternatively delete the tree keys from applySprites' override loop and let art.js be live.

### 2.2 Volume
Cliff faces (maps.js rasterColumn, cached):
- Strata: 3 bands per 6 px level, not 1 row: 2 px light cap band, 3 px mid, 1 px dark ledge; x-noise offset by the tile's i so neighbours do not align; every 4th level a 2 px ledge with a 1 px highlight above = readable rock courses.
- Ambient occlusion: bottom 3 px of every face −35 %; ground pixels within 4 px of a column base −20 % (rasterRect knows neighbour heights); same −20 % under the island's slab lip.
- Overhang lip: the top face overhangs the wall by 1 px (top face colour shifted 1 px outward) — the cue that makes a cliff heavy.
- Cast shadow: for every column, a baked wedge on the lower ground to the SW, length 0.6 x h x ZS px at 25 %. Trees get a LIVE directional shadow: cached skewed silhouette (silOf of the canopy, skew −30°, scale y 0.4, alpha 0.3), nearest 60 per frame; phones keep the ellipse.
- Height fog: in rasterRect mix the surface toward th.haze by 6 % per level below the map's max: free, and stacked plateaus read as distance.
Canopy over the hero:
- Trees become two objects: trunk (sorted as now) and canopy (key = trunk key + 0.5). In drawItem, if the hero's screen rect intersects the canopy rect and the hero's key < canopy key, draw the canopy at globalAlpha 0.35 (lerped over 0.2 s via a per-object fade field). One rect test per visible tree.
- Under-canopy light: hero light r x 0.8 and 2–3 dapple lights (r 24, a 0.3) at hashed offsets. The forest gets a roof.

### 2.3 Density (tools/maps/recipes/_forest.js, _vale.js, _cave.js; counts per 10x10 land tiles, enforced by the build.js audit)
- Off-path forest (dPath > 2.5): 12–18 trees (60 % tall, 30 % small, 10 % giant), 20–30 small clutter (grass 8–10, litter decals 6–8, rocks 2–3, mushrooms 3–4, logs/stumps 1–2).
- Corridor verge (0.6 < dPath < 2.5): 4–6 trees, 10–14 clutter; let r ≤ 0.22 props into the verge by lowering placeOk's margin from 0.55 to 0.15.
- Fight rooms: ≤ 3 trees on the rim, 0 colliders inside, 6–10 flat decals.
- Spacing: pine sp 1.5 -> 1.0, pine_young 0.95 -> 0.6, poplar/teal 1.5 -> 1.0; n x 2.5 in both rule files; trees in groups [2,5] spread 1.6 so they clump; one open glade per 15x15 (a rule with p = 0 inside a hashed disc) so density has rhythm.
- Ground decals (new OBJ_TYPES, no collider, drawn onto the chunk canvas after putImageData like drawCrystals, never in the y-sort): leaf litter x3, needle mat, pebble scatter, roots x4 (at trunk bases, p 0.5 per tree), moss patch, flower cluster, puddle, cobble edge = 16 kinds per zone. They replace the per-pixel flecks entirely.
- Roots and rocks at cliff feet: 1 per 3 tiles of wall base.

### 2.4 Borders — what the edge of a dream island should look like
Target image: a thick cake of rock and roots whose underside tapers into mist; the mist is a cloud sea carrying the island's shadow; waterfalls go off the lip into it and vanish; far islands float at eye level in a haze band; sky only above the horizon.
- Back edges (north/west): a face cannot be drawn in this projection, so give those coasts THICKNESS THAT FACES THE CAMERA: the generator (gen.js _levels / _coast) raises a 2–3-tile band along north/west coasts by +2..3 levels; its south/east walls render through the existing rasterColumn, and the existing wall(c) tree-line bias in the rules plants the rim. Every island becomes a bowl with a visible rim instead of a card. Keep a raster-side 2 px lip highlight + 1 px dark underline on every top edge.
- Front skirt: a 3-band skirt replaces the curtain: (1) slab 12 -> 18 px with 2 strata and a 2 px overhang lip; (2) rock lobes, 4–7 rounded masses per 100 px of edge (the existing 7 px `spike` buckets), each with its own lit/shade side, hanging roots 20–40 px with 2–3 forks, 1 crystal per 60 px; (3) fade: skirt ALPHA goes to 0 over the last 40 % so it dissolves into cloud instead of ending in a line.
- Island shadow on the cloud sea: a cached dark silhouette (the groundPreview silhouette, scaled 1.0 x 0.3, alpha 0.25) drawn under bg.sea: one drawImage per frame.
- Cloud sea: 2 layers of large soft tiles (256x96, built like buildFog but denser) at parallax 0.55/0.85, 3 px/s, alpha 0.75/0.95, plus the existing 3 front clouds. Move the far mountains DOWN so their base sits on the cloud horizon.
- Edge waterfalls: 6–10 white spray pixels at the lip (pooled), a cached mist puff at 70 % of the fall, fade out 20 px before the skirt ends.
- Gutters: 'v' becomes a chasm with faces on BOTH lips (the far lip via the raised-band trick), a cloud/mist floor and 1–2 non-walkable root silhouettes crossing it; 'k' becomes rock (th.under[1]) with stalactites along the lip and a 3 px glow of the section ambient; 'under' sections get a cave backdrop instead of the sky (THEME_PARTS.sky per section, built once).

### 2.5 Cached vs live (the rule the team follows)
Chunk (rasterRect/rasterColumn + post-putImageData sprites): ground, transitions, decals, cliff strata/AO/wedge shadows, skirt lobes/roots, gutter faces, height fog. Once per theme: cloud-sea tiles, island shadow, cave backdrop, light sprites, tree sheets, shadow silhouettes. Live: y-sorted sprites (trunks, canopies with alpha), ≤ 60 tree shadows, hero silhouette, falls/spray, glints, fog tiles, ≤ 40 lights, bloom. Nothing per-pixel; nothing re-rasterised because the hero moved.

## 3. Taller set pieces and vantage points

### 3.1 Building them in this tile/height system
The experiment (B_tall_summit.png, B_tall_from_south.png) proves the engine already renders and walks a 13-level stack (78 px) with no code change: rasterColumn handles any h, chunks absorb it (105 ms with 64 tall columns). What is missing is generator/art, plus one render fix:
1. Level rhythm: keep walkable's 1.05 rule; step 1 only on ramps/switchbacks; walls at step 3–4 (18–24 px). A 12-level climb = 3 plateaus of 4, not 12 ledges of 1 (the ziggurat in B_tall_from_south is step-1 everywhere). Ceiling 28 units until viewTiles' 190 px pad is raised (Spire to 30 with the pad at 240).
2. Stacked plateaus: gen.js stack(id, {x, y, levels: [0,4,8,12], r: [10,7,4.5,2.5], twist: 0.6}) written into Q after _levels (where walled rooms already go), each ring's centre offset by twist x r so the stack leans and walls are not concentric.
3. Switchbacks: switchback(id, {from, to, legs: 3, w: 2}) = a path with level holds per leg and 180° turns; at slope 0.6 a 12-unit climb is 20 tiles of path, 7 tiles of footprint with 3 legs. _pathsPass's Lipschitz profile and _stairs already emit the ramps and 's' tiles.
4. Ladders/roots: a 'root_ladder' object pair (12 px wide root rope, h x 6 px tall, column-keyed) with Interact = a 0.6 s climb that moves the hero z0 -> z1 along the sprite (the warpdoor/startWarp code path without the fade). A 4-level wall becomes passable with no ramp.
5. Drop-down ledges: a lip object with drop: true that lets walkable accept dh ≤ −4 for 0.3 s and plays fall + landing dust: one-way shortcuts for 0 tiles of path.
6. Summit "view" mode: on the summit trigger the camera eases 40 px down-slope, then after 0.6 s the reveal: fill m.mini.vis in a 24-tile radius (hud.js miniBuild's rev canvas), and a 2.5 s cinematic blits groundPreview(m) (already 1/4-res; Pinewood ~896 px wide) centred on the hero at 0.5x so ~60x60 tiles fit the 480x270 scene, with section insets (screens.js fullMapCanvas), shrine dots and the fog edge animating outward, then cuts back. ~1 ms/frame, no new raster. Desktop only (!PHONE): a true pull-back by rendering SCN at 960x540 for 2 s and downscaling (4x chunk draws, fine on a laptop).

### 3.2 Per-map signature tall piece and vantage point
- Whispering Vale (80x80) — The Dreamer's Cairn: the flat lvl-4 rift terrace (409 tiles, NE) becomes the first of three leaning plateaus 4/9/14 (84 px) joined by one switchback and one root ladder; the Elder Stone's lost light on top. Vantage: the Stone's Crown (h 14): full-Vale reveal, three rifts and both islets visible, first look over the cloud sea. Payoff: drop-down from 9 into the garden (0) = 25 s shortcut home; lore: the light was "set where the dream could see itself".
- Misty Pinewood (112x112) — Hawk's Rest bluff: a 3-plateau stack 4/10/16 (96 px) NE above r3 and the Clearing, walls at step 3, two switchback legs on the south face, a fallen giant pine (40-tile 'b' bridge at h 6) across the creek as the approach. Vantage: the bluff top looks DOWN into Bramblehorn's arena before you enter (pillars, gate, the boss asleep); forest-section reveal. Payoff: drop from 10 into the arena approach, shrine 3 moves up here; Pip: "From up here it's small. It isn't."
- Gloomspire Ruins — The Broken Stair: a collapsed tower, 5 plateaus 0/5/10/15/20 (120 px), gaps bridged by fallen pillars ('b' at height), root ladders, rubble decals. Vantage: the Belfry (h 20): the plaza's pillar names only read from above (the puzzle's answer). Payoff: drop to the Crypt door; the reliquary glows below as a lure.
- Sunken Grotto — The Tide Well: inverted, rim at h 18 and a spiral ramp DOWN to water at 0; stalactite "canopies" over the descent reuse the canopy-fade layer. Vantage: the rim lip on arrival reveals the whole cave, Mirelle's basin glowing. Payoff: tide (water tile height) opens/closes the lower shortcut; a ledge reachable only at low tide.
- Emberfall Caldera — The Rim Walk: a ring at h 24 (144 px) around the crater, the Anvil at 0, lava falls (drawFall recoloured) down the inner wall, wind on the outer skirt. Vantage: the rim's high point: reveal + the lava lights the hero from below (one big light a 0.4). Payoff: a slag chute (drop) into the Anvil's back door after the fight; the vault is under the rim.
- Sleepless Spire (96x128) — the Spire itself: 6 ledges 0/6/12/18/24/30 (180 px; raise viewTiles pad to 240), each an arena-sized plateau, Twin Vigils at 12 and 24. Vantage: every ledge; the summit reveal shows the previous zones as islets on the cloud sea (bg.islets recoloured per zone). Payoff: falling = respawn at the last ledge shrine; the Vigil Walk vault hangs under ledge 18 by a root ladder DOWN.

### 3.3 Camera and occlusion problems tall pieces create, and the fixes
Problems (verifiable in the B_* shots and render.js):
1. Hero behind a wall: columns sort by key i + j + 1; a 12-level wall one tile SE of the hero is 72 px tall and covers him. Nothing handles it today.
2. Camera (main.js updateCamera) locks to the hero with a fixed −12 px bias: on a summit you see 135 px of your own mountain and none of the view; at a wall's foot the wall fills the top of the frame.
3. Culling pads (viewTiles 80/40/190) are too tight above 28 levels and for 140 px giants near the top edge.
4. The hero's sort key is p.x + p.y + p.z*0.35 + 0.01 (render.js list push): at z = 13 that is +4.5 tiles, so he draws OVER any column up to ~4 tiles SE of him, including a 3-level wall he should be behind. Harmless at 2–4 levels, wrong at 12–16.
Fixes (render.js/main.js, ~1.5 agent-days, all live-layer):
1. Hero outline behind walls: after the sorted draw, if any column with key > hero key has a screen rect overlapping the hero rect, blit silOf(frame, '#9ef0ff') at alpha 0.4 + a 1 px darker inner silhouette (one drawImage each). Same for the targeted enemy.
2. Cutaway: those occluders (max 24/frame) drawn at globalAlpha 0.45 when overlapping the hero rect; a dithered look = column drawn into a 64x200 scratch canvas, then a cached 2x2 checker with 'destination-out', only while occluded. Phones: plain alpha.
3. Camera height bias: ty = −12 − min(36, (p.z − zBelow) x 2) where zBelow is the lowest height within 6 tiles in the facing direction (summit: lead 36 px down-slope; wall foot: lead up by 0.5 x wall px, max 24), through the existing lerp k.
4. viewTiles pads mTop 40 -> 160, mBot 190 -> 240.
5. Sort key: use the z term only while airborne (dash/fall/drop); otherwise plain x + y, so standing on a plateau sorts like the plateau.
6. Generator validation: no fight room directly north of a wall taller than 4 levels, so combat never happens behind a cutaway.

## 5. What NOT to do
Perf traps
- No per-pixel work per frame: no getImageData, no ctx.filter, no per-frame recolouring. Static = rasterRect/rasterColumn + chunk cache. Never re-raster a chunk because the hero moved (cutaway and canopy fade are live alpha).
- No more full-screen fog layers (phones at one, desktop at three), no bloom above 1/4 res, LIGHT_MAX stays 40; a canopy dapple is 2–3 lights.
- Cast shadows: cached skewed silhouettes, capped (60 desktop / ellipse on phone); never uncapped transform+drawImage per tree.
- Vantage zoom-out on phones = the 1/4-res preview blit; never render the scene at 4x area on a phone; do not add a camera-scale factor to worldToView.
- Heights above 28 without raising viewTiles pads (columns pop); above 35 cannot be stored.
- No WebGL batcher for any of this (DESIGN 26.4): nothing here is draw-call bound.
- sprites.js stays under ~1.5 MB of data-URIs (phone boot decode): freeze only placed species, ≤ 36 sheets per zone.
Style traps
- Noise is not detail: no per-pixel speckle, no 3.5 % confetti, no dither across more than one ramp step. If a 1/4-scale downscale turns to grey mush, it failed.
- Step-1 terraces everywhere = ziggurats (B_tall_from_south.png). Walls are 3–4 levels; ramps are rare and deliberate.
- Lollipop trees: a single blob with a full outline is what the client called ugly; three masses, rim light, roots, no full outline.
- Do not let grade + vignette + bloom flatten the mid-ground; keep 5 value bands per shot, cliff faces darkest, sky lightest.
- ONE skyline per map, visible from the previous landmark; tall pieces everywhere destroy the reading.
- No fights behind cutaways or under dense canopy.

## 6. Creativity, and a more refined, beautiful tileset with more variety (client's extra request)

### 6.1 Process: how the studio pushes creativity in maps
1. Mood board per zone (docs/art/<zone>.md, one page, written BEFORE any recipe change): 3 reference words (Vale "lullaby, lantern, altitude"; Pinewood "cathedral pines, amber dusk, something big asleep"; Ruins "drowned bells, rust-red moon"; Grotto "bioluminescent tide, held breath"; Caldera "forge wind, black glass"; Spire "vertigo, white silence"), a 6-swatch palette (sky, mid-ground, shadow, light accent, water, danger), a one-line shape language (Vale soft ovals and drifting horizontals; Pinewood tall verticals and drooping diagonals; Ruins broken verticals and arches; Grotto hanging and pooled curves; Caldera jagged and cracked; Spire thin spikes and long drops), and 2–3 marked-up game screenshots of what to keep. Agents are briefed from this page.
2. Signature-moment rule: every SECTION names, in its recipe header, the one moment the player screenshots and the camera position it is seen from (shots-big.js captures it). No moment, not done. Vale hub = first look over the cloud sea from the Crown; Hollow = roots overhead lit by one blue lantern; Clearing = the arena seen from the bluff with the boss asleep.
3. Landmark briefs: a landmark is (a) > 80 px tall on screen, (b) unique on its map, (c) visible from the previous landmark (DESIGN 25.1), (d) carries a light or motion. Brief = 5 lines: name / silhouette word / height in levels / what it tells the player / its sound. Today's gen.js landmark (three giants + a sunbeam) fails (b): it repeats 4 times on the Pinewood.
4. Variation budgets per 10x10 land tiles, enforced in build.js --audit: ≥ 3 tree species, no identical (type, variant) within 3 tiles, ≥ 2 ground types, ≥ 4 decal kinds, ≥ 1 height change per 15 tiles of main path, 1 landmark per 30 tiles, 1 glade per 15x15.
5. Anti-samey generator rules: (a) a hand-drawn 8x8 ASCII macro-mask per map (land/void/high/water) that _coast and _levels must respect, so each island's silhouette is authored; (b) a room-shape vocabulary of 6 (round, blob, square, crescent, split-by-water, ring-around-pillar), ≥ 4 per map; (c) asymmetry: the main path never climbs where the branch climbs; (d) one broken rule per section (a treeless room, a creek-bed path, a fallen-tree bridge); (e) per-zone meander/every/step profiles so corridors have a different handwriting; (f) the elevation story: every outer section rises or falls at least twice and the boss is seen before it is reached.

### 6.2 How to get a refined, beautiful tileset
Tile families per zone (a "tile" is a rule in maps.js, rendered at raster time):
- Base: 3 ground types (Vale grass/meadow/moss; Pinewood needle floor/grass/moss; Ruins broken flag/grass/ash) x 4 micro-variants chosen by hash(i, j) PER TILE (variant = macro-noise phase + 2–3 decorative pixels), not per pixel.
- Transition: 47-blob autotile computed in rasterRect from the 8-neighbour mask of the dominant type (mask -> one of 47 cases -> a scalloped 3–5 px tongue of the lower-priority type with a 1 px darker line). Priority per zone: water > path > stone > moss > meadow > grass. Replaces the vnoise type jitter. +10–15 % raster.
- Edge: 2 px lit lip + 1 px dark underline wherever a top meets a lower height or void; a 1 px bright wet edge on banks (replacing the −0.28 darkening).
- Cliff face: the 3-band strata family with per-zone courses (Vale soft layered chalk + roots; Pinewood dark granite with moss caps; Ruins cut masonry, 1 missing block in 5; Grotto wet highlight line; Caldera columnar basalt, 4 px verticals; Spire white marble veins).
- Top caps: grass overhang, stone cap, moss cap; 'x' flagstones in 3 damage states.
- Decal layer: 16 kinds per zone (2.3).
Palette ramps with hue shift: 7 steps; shadows shift 15–25° toward the zone's cool (Vale violet, Pinewood blue, Caldera deep red), lights 10–20° toward its warm (Vale pink, Pinewood amber); saturation peaks at steps 4–5; the lightest step is never pure white except water sparkle. Vale grass today #1d4a4e -> #cfeaa0 (straight); proposed #22284a, #1f4a52, #2c6b58, #47925e, #74b86a, #a6d47e, #e0eaa4. Same treatment for stone/earth/under so cliffs stop being grey.
Dithering rules: ordered 2x2 Bayer only between adjacent ramp steps and only in steps 2–5; none on lips or the darkest step. Noise scales: macro 0.14/tile keep; medium 0.7/tile keep with amplitude halved to 0.3; nothing finer than 2 px. Detail comes from decals and variants, never pixel hash.
Per-tile micro-variation: 4 variants per base x 47 transitions x decals means the eye never sees a repeat; paths 3 variants (cobble, worn, overgrown).
Seasonal / dream-distortion variants: per-section palette overrides (sec.grass/sec.stone remaps in surfaceColor, keyed by the existing sectionAt) plus the existing lightmap ambient: Moon Pond at night (blue-shifted, 15 % desaturated), Dream Arena (hue +40°, 2 px chromatic offset baked), Spire white-out (ramps mixed 40 % to #e8e4ff). One distortion per map, story-triggered, chunks re-raster lazily (LRU).
Water, waterfall, mist: water stays baked + a live caustic overlay (64x64 looping light mask at 'overlay' over water rects in view, 4 px/s, 2 offset frames) + the existing glints; banks get baked foam; falls get spray + 3 column variants (drawFall already animates). Mist: a static low band baked at the skirt; live 1-layer mist only in 'under' sections.
Biome blending at zone borders: a 6-tile band where surfaceColor lerps the two zones' ramps index-wise by a noise-warped distance, decal sets unioned with distance-weighted p, both species lists for trees. Shows at the Vale rift arrival and the Caldera/Grotto seams.

### 6.3 Variety targets per zone, and procedural vs hand-authored
Per zone: base tiles 3 x 4 = 12 (+3 path variants); transitions 47 x 3 priority pairs computed; decals 16; props 20 types (6 rocks, 3 logs/stumps, 4 lights, 3 story props, 4 clutter); tree species 3 x 6 variants x 2 sizes = 36 sheets + 3 giants; cliff courses 1 family x 2 variants; 1 dream-distortion palette.
Recommendation: stay procedural-in-code and freeze approved output to sheets. No human artist; agents iterate well in code; the pipeline already has both halves (art.js builders -> headless Chromium toDataURL -> js/sprites.js; the editor's pixel editor + canvasToSprite/world.sprites for touch-ups on small props). Pros: one lighting rule across 200+ sprites, one change fixes every tree, variants free; nothing a model hand-pixels at 64x140 beats a good builder. Cons: builders drift to blobby and samey — mitigated by the acceptance bar and by hand-authored ASCII strings (as the chest/lantern/tablet already are) for ≤ 24 px props with character (signs, braziers, keys, idols). Rule: procedural for ≥ 32 px organic/architectural (trees, cliffs, ground, roots, skirts); ASCII hand-strings for ≤ 24 px props; freeze whatever is approved so a later refactor cannot silently change the look.

### 6.4 Hero zone first, and the acceptance bar
Hero zone: Misty Pinewood OUTER section. It has the strongest ingredients (pines, dusk sun, god rays, mist), it is the Act I slice, and it exercises every family (needle/grass/moss, dirt path, creek + ford, 2-level plaza, cliff-walled chamber, coast, gutters, the new bluff). Polish it to the bar, then port the rules to the Vale (which must look as finished as the forest; today it does not).
Acceptance bar, judged on the 6 fixed camera positions in tools/maps/shots-big.js, same positions every time:
1. Silhouette test: downscale to 120x68; sky, cliff faces, ground and trees still read as 4 separate value bands.
2. Value bands: luminance histogram shows ≥ 5 clusters; the darkest 10 % of pixels are cliff/trunk/shadow, the brightest 5 % are sky/light sources, never ground.
3. Speck count: isolated pixels differing > 60 luminance from all 4 neighbours < 50 per 480x270 frame (today: hundreds to thousands).
4. Density: in a non-fight frame ≥ 10 trees and ≥ 20 clutter/decals; no two identical (type, variant) sprites adjacent.
5. Edges: no top surface touches the sky without visible thickness (raised rim or face); every cliff shows ≥ 3 strata and an AO foot.
6. Height: ≥ 3 height levels visible in 4 of 6 shots; the signature landmark in frame in every shot.
7. Trees: three leaf masses and a rim light visible at 2x; no full outline; the hero under a canopy with the fade working in at least one shot.
8. Perf gate: engine-tour phone pass p95 ≤ 25 ms after the pass and ≤ 20 ms before M4 ships; no 256 px phone chunk over 120 ms.
Each PR attaches the 6 shots and the 8 numbers; the director compares against the previous set, not against taste.
